**For most local-business contexts in 2026, GEO and AEO are functionally the same thing — both describe the work of making a business retrievable and citable by AI assistants — but the labels come from different intellectual traditions and emphasize slightly different sub-disciplines. GEO ("Generative Engine Optimization") emerged from the research community studying how generative models produce answers; it emphasizes content shaping for the generation step. AEO ("Answer Engine Optimization") emerged from the practitioner community optimizing for answer engines specifically (Perplexity, ChatGPT, Google AI Overview); it emphasizes the retrieval and citation steps. In practice, the work each label describes overlaps about 80 percent. The remaining 20 percent matters for some businesses, doesn't for others. For most local services businesses, the distinction is mostly noise.**

The terminology problem is a sign the category is young. Established disciplines have settled vocabulary; emerging ones don't. AEO, GEO, AIO (Artificial Intelligence Optimization), LLMO (Large Language Model Optimization), and a half-dozen other labels are all competing for the same conceptual territory. Here's the honest map.

## Where the labels actually come from

### GEO

"Generative Engine Optimization" was coined and formalized by a research team led by Pranjal Aggarwal and Vinay Murahari, published in a widely-cited 2023–24 paper that framed the optimization problem around the generation step in modern AI assistants. The paper introduced concrete techniques — citation-rich content, source diversity, statistic-heavy framing — and showed they measurably increased visibility in generative-AI answers.

GEO became the preferred term in the academic and large-enterprise SEO communities because it tracks closely to how researchers describe the underlying mechanic. Enterprise SEO consultancies (Stackmatix, SEM Nexus, several Big Four advisory shops) tend to use GEO.

### AEO

"Answer Engine Optimization" predates GEO as a term, with usage going back to roughly 2019–20 in discussions of how to optimize for voice assistants and Google's answer boxes. The term gained renewed relevance in 2023–24 as the same fundamental work expanded to cover the new generation of AI assistants (ChatGPT, Claude, Perplexity, Gemini).

AEO became the preferred term among local-SEO practitioners and small-agency operators because it ties cleanly to a buyer's mental model — they're asking an "answer engine" a question, and the agency is optimizing for being the cited answer.

### Other variants

A handful of labels show up in the wild but are mostly noise:
- **AIO** (AI Optimization) — too generic; could mean anything from prompt engineering to AI-tool-stack consulting
- **LLMO** (LLM Optimization) — narrower than AEO/GEO; usually refers specifically to the generation step in LLMs without the broader retrieval pipeline
- **AI SEO / Search AI Optimization** — usually marketing-shop renaming of standard SEO with AI features added on top

When you see these labels in agency proposals, they usually indicate either an agency early in the category trying to differentiate, or a re-skinning of existing SEO work to capture a hot-keyword market.

## Where GEO and AEO actually differ in emphasis

About 80 percent of the work is identical regardless of label:

- Structured-data markup (Schema.org)
- Entity disambiguation across platforms
- Citation-worthy content (specific facts, ranges, named entities)
- FAQ deployment with proper schema
- Review velocity and management
- Multi-engine citation tracking
- Content production targeting buyer-intent prompts

The 20 percent that diverges:

- **GEO leans more into content shaping at the generation level** — how to structure paragraphs so the AI model is more likely to quote them in answers. Heavier emphasis on statistics, named studies, structured argument blocks.
- **AEO leans more into the retrieval-and-citation operations** — which platforms feed which AI assistants, what the citation patterns look like, how to measure share, when to expect lift after specific work types.

For most local services businesses, the retrieval-and-citation operations side (AEO emphasis) is the higher-leverage work because the local-business content has more obvious citation-worthy patterns (cost ranges, service area, materials, regulations). The generation-shaping side (GEO emphasis) matters more for thought-leadership content where the model is being asked to summarize complex reasoning across multiple sources.

## Which framing actually matters for a local business

For a roofer in Phoenix or a custom home builder in Chandler or a plumber anywhere — the framing barely matters. The work is operationally identical regardless of which label the agency uses. Pick the agency based on quality of work, not on which acronym they preferred in their proposal.

For an enterprise client running multi-million-dollar campaigns across many AI surfaces and many markets, the framing matters somewhat more — because the methodology emphasis (GEO vs AEO) tracks to which agency type tends to specialize in their needs. But that's a small share of the market.

For agencies and operators *in* the category, the framing matters as a positioning question: which intellectual community do you anchor in, which research do you cite, which conferences do you go to. That's an internal-positioning concern, not a client-facing decision.

## What to ask an agency about this

If an agency uses one label heavily and dismisses the other, that's worth probing. The dismissive position usually indicates they came from one community and aren't engaging with the other's work. The work itself doesn't care about the label, so an operator who's deeply read in only one tradition is leaving roughly 20 percent of the available techniques on the table.

A useful test question: "If I wanted to dig into the technical literature on this work, which papers or research would you point me to?" An operator versed in the full landscape can name resources from both the GEO research community (the original Aggarwal-Murahari paper, follow-up work in the same lineage) and the AEO practitioner community (case studies, agency methodology blogs, conference talks). An operator versed in only one usually names sources from only one side.

## The honest summary

You can use the labels interchangeably for most local-business purposes and lose nothing meaningful. Treat the distinction the same way you treat "marketing" vs "growth" — different vocabulary, mostly the same underlying work, surface emphasis differences that matter to practitioners but rarely change what gets done. The agency you should hire is the one doing good work, regardless of which label they put on it.
