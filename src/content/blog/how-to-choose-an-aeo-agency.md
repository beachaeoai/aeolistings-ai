**The honest test for an AEO agency in 2026 isn't credentials or case studies — it's whether they can show you their own citation share across the major answer engines, and whether their measurement approach survives a fifteen-minute conversation. Agencies that pass that test cluster around six attributes: published prompt-set methodology, named experts as authors on their own content, willingness to quote specific recurring pricing on a first call, founders who personally do strategy work, multi-engine tracking (not just Google AI Overview), and a clear answer to "what happens when a model update breaks our work." Agencies that fail tend to fail visibly within those six.**

The category is young enough that the agencies in it range from one-person operators with deep expertise to large generalist marketing shops that added "AEO" as a service line last quarter. Telling them apart matters because the work is meaningfully different between them.

## What real AEO expertise actually looks like

A useful test: ask the agency to walk you through their own citation share, by prompt category, across ChatGPT, Claude, Perplexity, Gemini, and Google AI Overview. The honest agency has these numbers, will share them, and can talk about which prompts they win, which they lose, and what they're doing about the losses. The dishonest agency will either deflect or substitute Google search rankings as a proxy.

The reason this test works: AEO measurement is the hard part of AEO. Anyone can produce content. Knowing whether that content is actually getting cited — and where the citations are coming from in the model's retrieval blend — requires methodology that most agencies haven't built yet. If they can't measure their own performance credibly, they can't measure yours.

## Six attributes of agencies worth talking to

### 1. Published methodology, not "proprietary process"

Agencies worth hiring describe how they measure citation share, what surfaces they track, and how often they refresh the data. "Proprietary process" usually means "we don't actually have a process" or "we ran one experiment in 2024 and are still extrapolating from it."

### 2. Named experts as authors on their content

Look at the agency's own blog. Are posts authored by named humans with bylines, or attributed to "the team" or unsigned? AI assistants increasingly weight author identity as a citation signal — and agencies that don't apply this to themselves usually don't apply it to clients either.

### 3. Willingness to quote recurring pricing on the first call

Honest agencies tell you the recurring monthly range inside the first conversation. They might caveat ("depends on competitive density in your market"), but they'll give you a number. Agencies that refuse to talk pricing until "the second meeting" are usually selling enterprise-tier pricing to small-business clients and need to do discovery on what you can afford before they quote.

### 4. Founders or named experts personally do strategy work

For a one- to ten-person agency, the founder is usually the strategic asset. Find out who is actually going to be on your engagement, by name, before signing. Some shops use a senior founder for sales and a junior account manager for delivery — that's fine if it's transparent, problematic if it's hidden.

### 5. Multi-engine tracking, not just Google AI Overview

Google AI Overview gets disproportionate attention because it shows up directly in search results. But Perplexity, ChatGPT, Claude, and Gemini are increasingly where high-intent buyers go before they ever open a Google search. An agency that only tracks Google AI Overview is undercovering the surface area where your real competition lives.

### 6. A clear answer to "what happens when a model update breaks our work"

Models change. Retrieval blends shift. Citation patterns reshuffle on a six-to-twelve-week cadence. Ask the agency: what happens when a model update wipes out citations we currently have? How fast can you tell, and what does response look like? Good answers describe weekly monitoring, defined response procedures, and a built-in expectation that ranking shifts happen. Bad answers express surprise at the question.

## Three red flags that should end the conversation

### "We guarantee top-three AI ranking in 90 days"

AI doesn't rank. Citation share is contested across multiple engines and shifts with model updates. Anyone guaranteeing a specific outcome is either misrepresenting how the technology works or measuring something cheaper than citation (usually Bing rankings or featured snippets) and calling it AEO.

### Bundled pricing with no breakdown

A single monthly number with no line items makes scope creep invisible and renegotiation impossible. Worse, it usually conceals that the foundation work (which should be one-time) is being amortized into the recurring retainer forever.

### Generic deliverables, not specific to your market

Ask: "What are the top ten buyer-intent prompts you'd start tracking for my business?" An agency that can't produce that list inside the discovery call doesn't have a clear-enough read on your market to do the work well. Generic deliverables produce generic results.

## Questions to ask on a discovery call

A short checklist worth running:

- What's your citation share across the major answer engines for your own brand right now?
- Can I see a sample monthly client report (anonymized is fine)?
- Who, by name, will be on my engagement?
- What's the recurring monthly range, and what's the one-time foundation cost?
- How often do you re-tune the prompt set, and what triggers a retune?
- What's your churn rate on retainer clients?
- What happens if a model update tanks my citations — how fast do I know, and how do you respond?

You're not just buying execution. You're buying judgment about a market that's changing faster than most agencies can keep up with. The discovery call is your chance to test for that judgment before the contract.

## The one question worth asking last

After everything else, ask: "What do you think we don't need from your service?" An honest agency will tell you. They'll point to something they could sell you that wouldn't help, and explain why. That's the answer worth weighting heaviest — because the question separates agencies that are trying to sell you a package from agencies that are trying to help you win.
