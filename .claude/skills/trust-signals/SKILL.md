# Trust signals — sourcing, displaying, surfacing

## Why this is usually the #1 priority finding

Across every visibility audit, "missing trust signals" is consistently in the top 3 issues — and "placeholder testimonials" is the single most damaging item that appears on real production sites. A $1M+ buyer (or even a $20K roof buyer) does not need to be told the team is "trusted, professional, and dedicated" — they need to see verifiable proof.

The fix isn't writing better marketing copy. It's sourcing real signals and displaying them properly.

## The trust-signal stack (12 signals worth surfacing)

For most service businesses, the goal is to display as many of these as legitimately apply:

| Signal | Where to display | Source / how to get |
|---|---|---|
| ROC / state license # | Footer (sitewide), contact page, every quote | Already exists — just show it |
| Bonded / insured language | Footer, About, contact | Verify limits with broker, then state plainly |
| BBB accreditation | Footer + About | Apply through BBB, ~$700/yr typical |
| Industry association memberships (NAHB, HBACA, NARI, ARCA, IEC, NECA) | Footer + About + service-specific pages | Apply through each — most have local chapters |
| Manufacturer certifications (GAF Master Elite, Owens Corning Preferred, Tesla Certified, Qmerit, Generac PowerPro) | Service-specific pages where relevant | Apply through manufacturer portals |
| Press logos | Homepage band | Source from any publication that's covered the business |
| Awards (Best of Houzz, Phoenix Business Journal Top 10, regional Best Of) | Homepage + About | Submit through Houzz/PBJ portals + apply for regional awards |
| Real testimonials with named clients | Homepage + service pages + city pages | Email/text 10–20 recent clients; offer a review-link or pull from existing platforms |
| Case studies with location/scope/budget | Dedicated portfolio pages | See `case-study` skill |
| Named-expert / founder bio | About page + author bylines on blog | Write a real bio with credentials, photo, podcast/press appearances |
| Podcast appearances / industry-press features | About page + relevant service pages | Embed players or link with schema |
| Years in business + counter signals | Hero + footer | The actual founding date (be honest about company tenure vs. team experience) |

## Step 1 — Replace placeholder content (Day 1, non-negotiable)

If the site has *any* of these in production, fix today:

- "Enter quote here. — First LastTitle of Comany"
- "Project Title" / "Excerpt here" / "Lorem ipsum"
- Stock testimonial photos with no name
- "Named Personnel: Name1, Name2" with no bio detail
- Default theme content from a WordPress / Wix / Squarespace template

This is non-negotiable. A $1M+ buyer who sees placeholder copy on the homepage is gone in the first 10 seconds.

## Step 2 — Source real testimonials (Day 1–7)

The fastest path to 6–10 real testimonials:

1. **Pull existing reviews from off-platform sources** — Yelp, Google, Houzz, HomeAdvisor, BBB, Angi, Nextdoor. With permission (most platforms allow re-use with attribution), embed the verbatim review with reviewer name + platform + date.

2. **Email/text 15–20 recent clients** with a short, specific ask:
   > "Hi [name] — I'm updating our website with real client feedback. Would you mind sharing 2–3 sentences about your experience with us, especially [specific service]? If you're comfortable, can I include your first name + city + the project type? Reply yes/no, no follow-up needed."
   Expect 30–50% response. 6 responses = 6 testimonials.

3. **Capture testimonials at job-completion** going forward — make this part of the close-out workflow.

### Testimonial display rules

- **Always include the reviewer's first name + last initial + city + project type.** "Sarah K., Mesa — tile reroof" beats "Sarah" beats "Anonymous."
- **Quote verbatim** when possible — don't smooth out the language.
- **Photo only with explicit permission** — don't pull a stock photo to "represent" the reviewer.
- **No more than 6–10 on the homepage** — beyond that, link to a dedicated reviews page.
- **Mark with Review schema** *only if real* — never apply Review/AggregateRating schema to fake content.

## Step 3 — Display the license + accreditation stack (Day 1)

Sitewide footer should include:

```
Eco Roofing Solutions, LLC
75 W Baseline Suite 19, Gilbert, AZ 85233
(480) 695-7736  |  info@example.com
AZ ROC #XXXXXX  |  Licensed, Bonded & Insured
BBB Accredited Since 2020  |  ARCA Member
```

This block should be in the layout template — every page, footer, never page-specific copy. Match the formatting on Google Business Profile, the website schema, and any quote/invoice templates.

## Step 4 — Pursue association memberships (30-day workstream)

For builders, roofers, electricians, plumbers — local industry associations are real ranking signals (directory citations) and real conversion signals (BBB-style trust badges).

**Build sector:**
- NAHB (National Association of Home Builders) — automatic with HBACA
- HBACA (Home Builders Association of Central Arizona) — local chapter
- NARI (National Association of the Remodeling Industry)
- AIA referral relationships (architects refer trusted builders)

**Roofing:**
- ARCA (Arizona Roofing Contractors Association)
- The Roofing Institute
- NRCA (National Roofing Contractors Association)

**Electrical:**
- IEC of Arizona (Independent Electrical Contractors)
- NECA Arizona Chapter
- IBEW (if union-trained)

Each application is straightforward and most are sub-$1,000/year. The visible-badge value typically pays back inside 6 months.

## Step 5 — Pursue manufacturer certifications (60–90 day workstream)

For categories where they exist, manufacturer certifications are powerful both for trust and for being cited as a recommended installer in manufacturer directories.

| Category | Certifications worth pursuing |
|---|---|
| Roofing | GAF Master Elite (top 2% nationally), Owens Corning Platinum/Preferred, CertainTeed SELECT ShingleMaster, Malarkey Emerald |
| Electrical | Tesla Certified Installer, Qmerit, ChargePoint Preferred, Square D / Schneider Electric, Eaton, Generac PowerPro |
| HVAC | Carrier President's Award, Trane Comfort Specialist, Lennox Premier Dealer |
| Plumbing | Bradford White Premier, Rheem Pro Partner |
| Solar | SunPower Master Dealer, Tesla Powerwall Certified Installer |

Applications take time — most have training requirements, volume thresholds, or background checks. Start the process in week 1; expect badges in 60–120 days.

## Step 6 — Surface the named expert (Day 7–14)

For service businesses, named experts increasingly drive AI-search citations. AI assistants weight content with verifiable named authorship more heavily.

For each business, write/update:
- **An About page** with the founder's bio, headshot, credentials, years in industry (vs. company tenure), notable projects, and any press/podcast appearances
- **Author bylines on every blog post** with the author's name, photo, and short credential line
- **Author schema** linking blog content to the named person

Example bio block:
> *Eric Perry is the third-generation owner of Eco Roofing Solutions and an AZ ROC #-licensed roofer with 25+ years of experience in the Phoenix Valley. He's been featured on [podcast/publication] and serves on the board of [association]. Specializing in foam and tile systems for the desert climate.*

## Step 7 — Embed press/podcast features (Day 7–14)

When the founder/business has been featured in podcasts or press, surface it prominently:

- Embed the player on the About page
- Add a "Featured In" press-logo band on the homepage
- Mark with `Article` schema linking back to the source
- Add the URL to the `sameAs` array in Organization schema

Most service businesses have 1–3 features that are completely unleveraged. The Cruz Development audit identified the Brad Leavitt podcast appearance as exactly this kind of underleveraged signal.

## Step 8 — sameAs schema (Day 14)

Once the off-platform footprint is settled, add a `sameAs` array to Organization schema linking:

- Google Business Profile URL
- Facebook page
- Instagram profile
- Houzz profile (for builders/remodelers)
- BBB profile
- LinkedIn company page
- Industry directory listings
- Podcast / press feature URLs
- Yelp profile

This consolidates the entity for search engines and AI assistants. Especially important for businesses with name-collision risk.

## Common failure modes

- **Hand-written testimonials** when real ones could be sourced — looks fine to humans, transparently fake to AI assistants and savvy buyers
- **Stock photos labeled as team members** — caught immediately by reverse image search
- **Aspirational claims** ("Industry-leading", "Award-winning") without specific named awards
- **License number listed once on the contact page** — should be sitewide footer
- **Big "BBB" logo with no actual accreditation** — Google + BBB cross-reference and penalize
- **Years-in-business mismatch** between company founding date and team experience — be precise

## Output format when running this skill

For a one-time engagement, deliver:
1. **Trust-signal audit** — what's currently displayed, what exists but isn't surfaced, what's missing
2. **Testimonial sourcing plan** — list of clients to contact, draft outreach copy
3. **Footer NAP/license block** ready to paste into the layout template
4. **Association/certification application plan** — which to pursue, in what order
5. **Named-expert content** — drafted bio, suggested headshot brief, author byline format
6. **Press/podcast embed plan** — where to surface what
7. **sameAs schema array** — populated for the entity
