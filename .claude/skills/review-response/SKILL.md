# Review responses — every review, within 48 hours

## Why this matters

Google explicitly tracks two signals: **owner response rate** and **owner response speed**. A 100% response rate within 48 hours is a real ranking factor for the Local Pack. Beyond ranking, an unanswered negative review is a louder signal to prospects than a thoughtful response would be.

For ongoing retainer work, responses are weekly hygiene. For a Week-1 GBP sweep, every existing review (positive AND negative AND mixed) needs a reply.

## Response patterns by star rating

### 5-star reviews

Don't just say "thanks." A 5-star reply has three parts:

1. **Specific thanks naming the service** — "Thanks for trusting us with the tile reroof on your Anthem home" beats "Thanks for the great review!"
2. **Acknowledge a specific moment** the reviewer mentioned — "Glad Brandon was able to get the panel upgrade done before the holiday weekend"
3. **Forward-looking warmth** — "Anytime you need us for the next project, we're here"

**Length:** 30–60 words. Longer reads as performative.

### 4-star reviews

Same three parts, plus an honest acknowledgment of what could have been better:

1. Specific thanks
2. Honest "we hear you" on the deduction (timing, communication, etc.)
3. Concrete improvement note ("we've added a 24-hour-before-arrival text confirmation")

Don't fight the 4 stars. Don't ask for them to update. The acknowledgment IS the value.

### 1–3 star reviews

The hardest and most important. A real reply has four parts:

1. **Specific empathy** — name the issue the reviewer raised, in their language
2. **The fact pattern from the business's perspective** — what happened, calmly stated, no defensiveness
3. **Specific resolution offered** — direct contact name + phone + email (not "please contact us")
4. **No counter-attack on the reviewer** — even if the review is unfair

**Length:** 80–150 words. Take the time.

### Reviews that mention competitors or pricing comparisons

Don't engage on price comparisons. Acknowledge the experience, redirect to value (warranty, materials, named experts).

### Reviews that name a specific employee

**Always name the employee in the reply** when the reviewer named them positively. "We're glad Brandon could help" — both for the employee's morale signal and for the AI/human signal that real people work here.

When the reviewer named an employee negatively, address professionally: "We've spoken with [name] about the experience and are reviewing our process for [issue area]." Don't throw the employee under the bus publicly.

## Response time targets

- **Within 48 hours** for all reviews (the Google ranking factor)
- **Within 24 hours** for any negative reviews (damage control + public-facing trust)
- **Same day** for reviews that mention a current job in progress

For ongoing retainer work, set up a daily-check ritual. Don't batch into weekly — the time-decay matters.

## Templates (starting points, customize each)

### 5-star, residential service

> Thanks for trusting us with [service] on your [city/neighborhood] home, [first name]. Glad [specific detail from review — e.g., "Brandon was able to walk you through the panel options on-site"]. Anytime you need us for the next project, we're here. — [Owner name], [Brand]

### 5-star, commercial

> Appreciate the kind words, [first name]. [Specific detail — e.g., "Tenant improvements on the Mill Ave space were a real challenge with the existing wiring, and the team enjoyed the puzzle"]. Reach out anytime — we're glad to be on the call list. — [Owner name], [Brand]

### 4-star

> Thanks for the honest feedback, [first name]. You're right that [acknowledged issue — e.g., "the original timeline slipped on the underlayment repair"]. We've adjusted our [process change — e.g., "estimating process to flag underlayment-condition risk earlier"]. Glad the finished work [worked out / met spec] — and thanks for trusting us with the project. — [Owner name], [Brand]

### Negative — service issue

> [First name], thanks for taking the time to write this — and I'm sorry for the [specific issue]. From our records, [calm fact pattern — e.g., "the tech who arrived was scheduled for a 4-hour panel inspection but ran into existing wiring issues that extended the visit. We should have communicated the delay better."]. I'd like to make this right — please reach out to me directly: [name@email.com / direct phone]. — [Owner name], [Brand]

### Negative — pricing dispute

> [First name], thanks for the feedback. Our [service] quotes are based on [factor — e.g., "labor hours, materials, and code-required permits"], and the variance you're describing usually comes down to [specific factor]. I'd be happy to walk through your specific quote on a call — please reach out to me at [direct contact]. We stand by our pricing, but I want to make sure you have full clarity. — [Owner name], [Brand]

### Negative — communication issue

> [First name], you're right — we dropped the ball on [specific communication failure]. Since this happened, we've [process change — e.g., "implemented a daily-check-in text protocol for any job over 2 days"]. Doesn't undo your experience, but I appreciate the feedback and we'll be better for it. Please reach out if there's anything we can still do for the project. — [Owner name], [Brand]

## What NOT to do

- **Don't argue.** Even if the review is wrong. Public arguments hurt the brand more than the original review.
- **Don't accuse the reviewer of being a competitor / fake.** Even if you suspect it. Report through Google's process privately.
- **Don't use boilerplate** that's obviously the same on every review. AI assistants and prospects both notice.
- **Don't beg for stars** ("If you could update to 5 stars..."). Tacky. Counter-productive.
- **Don't apologize for things that aren't your fault** as a CYA move. Stand behind real work.
- **Don't ignore the reviewer's name.** Personalization matters more than length.

## For ongoing retainer (weekly cadence)

Weekly review-response workflow:
1. **Pull all new reviews** across Google, Yelp, BBB, Houzz, HomeAdvisor, Angi, Facebook, Nextdoor (whatever the business has presence on)
2. **Draft responses** following the patterns above
3. **Owner approval** — owner should approve all responses, especially negatives, before posting
4. **Post within 48 hours** of original review
5. **Monthly summary** to client: response rate, average response time, any reviews that warrant a follow-up call

For a Week-1 sweep, do the same for every historical review.

## Output format

For a single review-response request, deliver:
1. **The drafted response**
2. **Suggested response time** (now, within 24h, within 48h)
3. **Any escalation note** if the review warrants an offline call before responding

For a Week-1 sweep, deliver a doc with all historical reviews + drafted responses, ready for owner approval.
