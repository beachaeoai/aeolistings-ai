# Google Posts — 2/week, sustained

## Why this matters

In 2026, Google Posts are:
1. A top-tier Local Pack ranking signal (Google explicitly weights recent profile activity)
2. A direct GEO / AI Overview citation source (Google AI surfaces post content in conversational queries)
3. A "is this business actively running?" signal that affects click-through rate

Most service businesses post nothing on GBP. The gap between "posts nothing" and "posts twice a week consistently" is large. The gap between "posts twice a week" and "posts daily" is smaller. **Cadence beats volume.**

## Post types and when to use each

Google Posts have four formats. Each has its own use case:

### What's New (default workhorse)
The most flexible format. Use for: recent project, before/after photo, seasonal advisory, new service announcement, new product / partnership.

**Cadence:** 1/week minimum.

### Offer
Time-bound discount or promotion with a code, validity dates, and CTA. Best for: free-inspection campaigns, monsoon-prep specials, end-of-season tune-ups.

**Cadence:** 1/month, ideally tied to a real seasonal driver.

### Event
Date-bound event with start/end times. Best for: open houses, free in-person consultations, trade-show appearances, community events the business sponsors.

**Cadence:** As earned — when there's a real event.

### Product
Individual SKU with photo, name, price, description, CTA. Sits in the Products tab too. Best for: specific service offerings ("Free Drone Roof Inspection", "$350 Express Tile Repair").

**Cadence:** 5–10 entries seeded once during GBP optimization, then updated quarterly.

## Anatomy of a strong post

```
[Hero image: 1200×900px, real photo, geotagged, owner-shot]

[Title: 60 chars max — front-load the most specific service/location word]
Example: "Tile Reroof — Las Sendas, Mesa — Before/After"

[Body: 100–300 words — the AI-citation block]
What was done. Where (named neighborhood). What changed. What was distinctive.
Real numbers when possible. Named tech if appropriate.

[CTA button + link]
"Get a free roof inspection" → contact form
"Read the full project →" → case-study URL on website
```

**Key rule: the post's value is its body content, not its CTA.** AI assistants pull from the body.

## Repurposing — 2x leverage per piece of work

For retainer clients producing weekly blog content, each blog post can become:
- 1 GBP post (snippet + link to full post)
- 1 Instagram post (image + caption)
- 1 Facebook post (cross-post from Instagram or unique copy)

This is essentially free content reuse — write once, distribute four ways. Don't post the exact same copy verbatim across surfaces (Google may flag duplicate content), but the substance, photos, and structure can carry through.

## What makes a post AI-citable

Same rules as blog posts (see `aeo-blog-post`):
- **Specific numbers / ranges / timeframes** in the body
- **Named local entities** (neighborhood, city, permit office, named expert)
- **Concrete decision-relevant facts** — what a buyer would want to know
- **Direct sentences** — no marketing puffery

Bad post:
> *"Just finished another beautiful project! Our team really takes pride in quality work and customer service. Contact us today for your roofing needs!"*

Good post:
> *"Just finished a 2,400 sqft tile reroof in Las Sendas, Mesa. Original underlayment from 2002 had reached the end of its lifespan — we replaced it along with the ridge cap. 4-day turnaround, HOA color-matched tile, 25-year warranty active. The upper-bound budget for projects like this in Mesa runs $20K–$24K — happy to walk you through the math if you're in a similar 2000-built home."*

The good version contains 6 facts, 1 location, 1 timeframe, 1 cost range. The bad version contains 0 facts.

## Image rules

- **Always include a real photo** — never a stock graphic, never a quote-card with text
- **Use a 1200×900 (4:3) format** — Google's preferred aspect ratio
- **Geotag** if the image has GPS data
- **Owner / employee photo > job-site photo > before-after** in priority order
- **No photo** = much lower engagement and (anecdotally) lower citation odds

## Cadence calendar (for retainer clients)

Plan a 90-day GBP post calendar:

- **8–10 What's New posts/month** — recent projects, seasonal advisories, blog post repurposing
- **1 Offer/month** — tied to season (monsoon prep, year-end, etc.)
- **1–2 Events/quarter** as relevant
- **Products quarterly review** — update prices, swap photos, add new offerings

Schedule in advance. Don't write Monday's post on Monday morning — write the week's posts on Friday for the following week.

## What NOT to post

- **Generic "Happy [holiday]" posts** — wastes a slot
- **AI-generated boilerplate** — "Did you know..." filler
- **Reposts of manufacturer marketing copy** — content-quality hit
- **Long marketing essays** — Posts have a soft 300-word limit before truncation
- **Anything with no image** — much lower engagement
- **Anything that's not actually about the business** — no inspirational quotes, no "team appreciation Friday"

## Output format

For a single post, deliver:
1. **The post title** (60 char max)
2. **The post body** (100–300 words)
3. **CTA button text + destination URL**
4. **Image brief** — what to shoot or which existing photo to use
5. **Suggested format** (What's New / Offer / Event / Product)
6. **Schedule / publish date**

For a weekly batch, deliver 2 posts ready to schedule. For a 90-day calendar, deliver a full editorial sheet.
