The headline finding

Eco Roof is collecting reviews on the wrong platforms.

31 Yelp reviews, hundreds-implied on HomeAdvisor, BBB-accredited since 2020 — and only 5 reviews on Google. The Google Business Profile is the surface that decides Local Pack visibility, where 80%+ of "roofing contractor near me" traffic flows. Reviews on Yelp and HomeAdvisor help with trust once a customer has found you. They don't help you get found in the first place.

Critical gaps — ranked by impact

Ten fixes that change Local Pack visibility.

In priority order. The first three are recoverable inside week one and account for the largest share of the visibility lift.

  • Reviews going to the wrong platform5 Google reviews after 25+ years; Yelp has 31, HomeAdvisor hundreds. Pointing the review-request workflow at Google instead unlocks more ranking lift than any other single change. Critical
  • Zero photos on the listingTop-ranking East Valley roofers run 100+ photos. Eco Roof has 0 — actively suppressing rankings and click-through. Existing project photos exist on the website and Yelp; they need to be on the GBP. Critical
  • No owner responses to reviewsGoogle explicitly tracks response rate and speed as ranking signals. Replying to all 5 existing reviews (positive and the mixed one) is a free week-one win. Critical
  • Likely missing 5–9 secondary categoriesTile, foam, flat, shingle, residential, commercial, repair — each maps to a valid Google category. Operating with one primary leaves 80% of category-driven discovery on the table. High
  • No Google Posts cadencePosts are now both a top-tier ranking signal and an AI Overview citation signal. With weekly blog content already produced, repurposing is essentially free. High
  • Eco / cool-roof angle invisible on the GBPThe brand's most defensible asset isn't in services, attributes, posts, or photos. The GBP is one of the highest-leverage surfaces for asserting that positioning. High
  • No Q&A seededOwner-posted Q&A behaves identically to FAQ schema for AI Overview citation. Same fix as the website FAQ rewrite, in a different surface. Medium
  • No identity / service attributesFamily-owned, third-generation (Eric Perry as named expert), free estimates, free drone inspections — each is a filter Google uses in conversational Maps queries. Medium
  • No Products entriesFoam systems, tile, cool-roof coatings, drone inspection — even for service businesses, AI Maps summaries pull from Products entries. Medium
  • Mixed review without an owner replyAn unanswered negative review is a louder signal than a thoughtful response acknowledging the issue — and AI assistants summarizing the brand will surface it as-is. Medium

90-day rollout

A steady, weekly cadence —
not a redesign.

Sequenced so the highest-leverage changes happen in week one, the cadence-building work fills weeks 2–4, and the velocity ramp compounds across days 30–90.

Week 1 · Quick wins
Foundation
  • Confirm primary category = Roofing Contractor
  • Add 5–9 secondary categories
  • Reply to all 5 existing reviews
  • Upload 30+ existing project photos
  • Add identity + service attributes
  • Update business description with East Valley + eco
  • Seed 5–7 owner-posted Q&A entries
Weeks 2–4 · Activate
Build cadence
  • Wire review-request workflow to Google specifically
  • Begin 2 Google Posts / week (mix of project + offer)
  • Add 5–8 Products entries with photos & pricing ranges
  • Photo cadence: 5+ new per week
  • Confirm service-area cities match website footer (cap 20)
Days 30–90 · Compound
Ramp velocity
  • Target 4+ new Google reviews / month — drives 5 → 20+ in 90 days
  • Sustained 2 posts / week (now the floor)
  • Sustained weekly photo cadence — target 75+ total
  • Q&A monitoring <48 hours
  • Track Local Pack visibility for "roofing contractor Mesa AZ", "roof repair Gilbert AZ", "tile roof Mesa", "foam roof Phoenix"

Crosswalk to the proposed Scope of Services

Bottom line

The GBP is recoverable in 90 days. Without redesigning anything.

Most of what's broken on this profile is broken because no one's been actively running it. The work is steady, weekly, and unglamorous — but it compounds, and it changes who shows up when an East Valley homeowner searches "roofer near me." That's the whole game.
