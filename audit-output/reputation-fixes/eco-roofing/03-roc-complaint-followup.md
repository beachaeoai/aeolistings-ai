# ROC Complaint Follow-Up — Closure Documentation Request

**Goal:** If the original ROC complaint has been resolved (or is in active resolution), get the closure documented in the public ROC record. Updated complaint status is what shifts the AI-Overview citation corpus, because AI assistants pull from current ROC data — not just the verbatim complaint text from however long ago.

**Important — voice and approval:** This is sensitive. Eric Perry has to lead this personally. The drafts below are starting points, not final language. Eric reviews, adjusts to fit the actual situation, and decides whether to send.

**Effort:** 1–2 hours of Eric's time to confirm the complaint reference number, draft the cover letter, and submit. ROC processing time varies — typically 2–6 weeks for a closure update to appear in the public record.

---

Before any outreach, Eric needs the specific complaint reference at hand:

1. Visit the AZ ROC contractor search: https://azroc.my.site.com/AZRoc/s/contractor-search

4. Identify the specific complaint that's surfacing in AI Overview (text begins *"DO NOT HIRE ECO ROOFING SOLUTIONS!"*)

If the complaint is already marked closed/resolved in the ROC record, that's good — but we may need to ask the ROC to make the resolution status more prominent or add a closure note. If it's still open, the path forward is different (see Path B below).

## Path A — If the complaint is already closed/resolved

The ROC's public record may show "closed" but the resolution narrative is often missing or thin, leaving the original complaint text as the dominant signal AI assistants pull. The fix: request that the ROC add a **resolution narrative** to the public record.

## Path B — If the complaint is still open or partially unresolved

Different situation, different approach. The right move is **active outreach to the customer** who filed the complaint — through the ROC mediation process, not directly — to find a path to resolution that closes the file.

This is materially harder and shouldn't be approached as a reputation-management exercise. The right framing internally: *"We have an unresolved customer issue. We owe that customer a real attempt at resolution. The reputation impact is downstream of that, not the point."*

### Suggested next steps

1. **Reach out to the assigned ROC investigator** (the contact person on the original complaint file). Confirm the current status, what mediation steps have happened, and what would constitute closure.
2. **Make a renewed offer of resolution** to the original customer through the ROC mediation process. The offer should be substantive — re-inspection, scope of repair work, named timeline, written commitment. If a refund is the right resolution, offer that.
3. **Document the offer in writing.** Whatever the offer is, get it on paper and send through the ROC mediation channel so it becomes part of the official record.
4. **If the customer accepts and the work / refund is completed:** the closure documentation flows automatically from the mediation process. Path A then applies.
5. **If the customer rejects:** the ROC's mediation process has a formal "good faith offer not accepted" disposition. That's still better than an open-status complaint, and it gets recorded in the public file.

### What NOT to do

- **Don't reach out to the customer outside the ROC channel.** Direct outreach on an active ROC complaint can be characterized as intimidation or interference. Stay inside the formal mediation process.
- **Don't post a public response anywhere** disputing the customer's account. Even if the account is wrong, public dispute amplifies the original complaint and looks defensive to future buyers and AI assistants alike.
- **Don't treat this as a one-pass effort.** ROC mediation often takes 2–3 rounds. Expect to be in this for 60–90 days.

## A note on framing

The reputation-fix package treats the ROC complaint as a citation-corpus problem. That's the AI / search angle — and it's real. But there's also an underlying customer-experience reality to it, and the customer-experience side has to lead.

If the original work wasn't right, owning that and making it right is what closes the loop properly — both for the specific customer and for the ROC record. If the original work was right but the customer was unreasonable, the ROC mediation process is designed to handle exactly that case — and the outcome of that process becomes the public record AI assistants read from.

In either case, the durable fix runs through doing the work, not around it. Eric is in the best position to judge which scenario applies and how to proceed.
