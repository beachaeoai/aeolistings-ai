Real credentials,

Stag Electric has the trust components a 2026 Phoenix electrician needs — two AZ ROC licenses, BBB accreditation, BuildZoom score 95, 5.0★ on HomeAdvisor with named-tech testimonials. The current single-page website doesn't give any of that surface area to be ranked or cited.

Stag Electric is a Mesa-based, owner-led residential + commercial electrical contractor founded August 2018 by Christopher Bradshaw. The team's actual differentiator is the wedge of panel upgrades, EV chargers, and troubleshooting — exactly the right service mix for the 2026 Phoenix market, where home electrification demand is accelerating. The credentials backing that positioning are real: AZ ROC #343438 + #322448, BBB Accredited since January 2024, BuildZoom score of 95 (top 22% of Arizona contractors), and a 5.0/5 HomeAdvisor rating across 6 verified reviews — including testimonials that name a specific tech ("Brandon").

The problem isn't credentials. It's that the live site is essentially a single page. The audit could only find one indexable URL — no service pages, no city pages, no FAQ, no schema, no blog. Stag's quote (issued 2026-04-29) commits Aeolistings to building exactly that infrastructure; this audit baselines what the work is starting from.

The bigger immediate issue is what's not merchandised. EV charger installation is one of the fastest-growing Phoenix-metro buyer queries, and Stag's service mix includes it — but there's no EV page, no Tesla / Qmerit mention. Castle Electrical & Lighting (Tesla Certified, Qmerit partner) currently dominates AI answers for "EV charger installer Phoenix." That lane is winnable inside 90 days — but only if the site exists.

Local SEO structure: weak. Single-page site means no internal linking, no service-page schema, no city-page coverage. The strongest signals (BBB, ROC, BuildZoom) aren't surfaced in indexed copy.

AEO readiness: weak. No FAQ block, no schema, no blog footprint. Queries Stag could win — "EV charger install cost Phoenix", "panel upgrade cost Mesa", "Tesla Wall Connector installer near me" — currently surface competitors. None of those answers are hard to write; the surface doesn't exist yet.

Five biggest opportunities, in priority order: (1) ship the multi-page site as scoped, with a dedicated EV charger page leading; (2) display ROC# 343438 in the footer of every page from day one; (3) rebuild the GBP and wire HomeAdvisor reviewers onto Google; (4) publish FAQPage + LocalBusiness + Service schema across the new pages; (5) pursue Qmerit and Tesla Certified Installer status — they unlock referrals competitors are currently capturing.

04 — Detailed Findings

What we saw,
section by section.

4.1  Local SEO structure

The single-page architecture means no city pages, no service pages, no FAQ block, no schema — a structural ceiling on every other AEO/local-search lever.

The homepage exists but no service-specific or city-specific landing pages were detected in the indexed URL set — only the root URL surfaces in search. By contrast, Castle Electrical has dedicated /service-areas/phoenix-az/, /service-areas/scottsdale-az/, and individual service pages; Just Right Electric has dedicated EV / panel / smart-home / lighting service pages plus a service-area index.

The bigger structural issue is the absence of multi-page architecture. With one indexable URL, every dimension downstream — service-page schema, city-page coverage, FAQ blocks, internal linking — is structurally impossible. The Aeolistings quote already commits to a 5–7 page build that fixes exactly this.

4.2  On-page optimization

Title tag and meta description audit pending — only a single page is indexed. URL pattern (root only) is consistent but offers no surface area for service / city splits. Internal linking is by definition zero (no other pages to link to). The full service-page architecture is in the quote scope and will create the linking graph.

4.3  Service page quality

The residential roofing page is adequate but thin — roughly 300–400 words of unique service description before transitioning into a blog feed. It explains the four material types at a high level and references Arizona's climate, but it does not include process / what-to-expect, pricing range, warranty terms, project timelines, embedded photos of completed projects, embedded testimonials, or page-specific FAQs.

Six concrete improvements:

  • Build a dedicated EV Charger Installation page with cost ranges ($1,096 median no-panel-work; $2,744 with panel upgrade), permit info, panel-compatibility table, Tesla Wall Connector mention, and 30% federal tax credit context.
  • Build a dedicated Electrical Panel Upgrade page with cost ranges ($1,500–$4,000), permit cost ($50–$300), typical timeline, and a section on common older-Mesa-home panel issues.
  • Build a Troubleshooting / Diagnostics page — "why is my breaker tripping in summer" is high-volume in monsoon season and currently unowned by Stag.
  • Build a Generator Installation page if Stag offers it — Generac PowerPro certification opens a referral pipeline most Mesa electricians don't have.
  • Build a Commercial Tenant Improvements page — Stag's existing TI work is one of two services already in the indexed copy, but has no dedicated page.
  • Build a Smart Home / Whole-Home Surge Protection page — emerging category, low competition in Mesa-area indexed content.

Detailed Findings, continued

4.4  Service area & local relevance

No city pages currently exist. For a Mesa-anchored business serving Mesa / Gilbert / Chandler / Tempe / Scottsdale / Apache Junction / Queen Creek / Phoenix, that's eight winnable city-search lanes with zero on-page surface area. Local proof on the indexed page is limited to the address (Mesa) and a generic "locally owned" line. Just Right Electric demonstrates the city-pages-as-asset pattern at Stag's stage and size.

4.5  Trust & conversion signals

What's working: phone visible, multiple CTAs, HomeAdvisor Top Rated / Elite Service badges, ARCA & Roofing Institute affiliations, named owner, 25+ years tenure, family-owned framing.

Gaps relative to competitors:

  • ROC license number not surfaced in the indexed footer copy — both AZ ROC #343438 and #322448 should be visible on every page (also a ROC compliance requirement for contractor advertising).
  • No manufacturer certifications. Castle Electrical's Tesla Certified + Qmerit positioning currently captures the highest-intent EV install referrals — a lane Stag can compete in once certified.
  • No transparent pricing. Just Right Electric publishes EV charger install starting prices ($459–$699) — strong both for conversion and AEO citation.
  • HomeAdvisor 5.0★ not surfaced on site. The 6 verified HomeAdvisor reviews + named-tech testimonials should be embedded with Review schema.
  • BBB accreditation badge not surfaced on the indexed page — Stag was accredited January 2024.

4.6  AEO & answer-engine readiness

AEO readiness is currently zero — no schema markup, no FAQ block, no AEO content footprint. Every Phoenix-electrician AI query (cost, EV install, panel upgrade, breaker troubleshooting, Tesla Wall Connector) currently surfaces competitors instead of Stag. The flip side: the underlying credentials Stag will mark up are stronger than what's visible on most competitor sites — the AEO catch-up is mostly mechanical.

Eight specific AEO improvements:

  • Build a citation-worthy FAQ block on the homepage and each service page — each answer 60–120 words with real numbers (cost ranges, permit timelines, voltage specs).
  • Add FAQPage schema markup to every page with a FAQ block — drives both Local Pack rich results and Google AI Overview citations.
  • Add LocalBusiness schema sitewide with the Gilbert NAP, service area, hours, and ROC license number.
  • Build a definitional EV Charger Installation hub page — own the "how much does it cost / how long does it take / what panel do I need" answers AI assistants surface.
  • Add transparent "starting at" pricing on the EV charger and panel-upgrade pages — standard practice in this category in 2026.
  • Add a clear, structured service-area block at the bottom of each service page (city + ZIPs).
  • Add named-expert content — a short bio block for Christopher Bradshaw + Brandon (the named tech). Named experts are an emerging AI-search trust signal.
  • Add "process" and "what to expect" step lists to each service page (estimate → permit → install → inspection).

08 — Strategic Notes

Where the upside is.

The biggest upside for Stag is not a pivot or a brand reinvention — it's putting the existing credentials onto a surface area that can be ranked and cited. Stag has the ROC numbers, the BBB accreditation, the BuildZoom 95, the 5.0 HomeAdvisor reviews, the named team, and the right service-mix wedge for the 2026 Phoenix market. Most of the visibility gap is unbuilt infrastructure, not strategic missteps.

A clean order of operations

Fix the leaks. Speak to AI. Build the content. Layer the polish.

  1. Ship the multi-page site first. Single-page presence caps every other channel. Service pages, city pages, FAQ blocks, and schema all need somewhere to live before any of them can compound.
  2. Then surface the trust signals you already have. ROC# 343438 in the footer of every page, BBB accreditation badge, BuildZoom 95, HomeAdvisor 5.0 — these compound the moment they're visible.
  3. Then rewrite the FAQ for AI. The change most likely to start producing AI-search citations on a 60–120 day curve. Substantive answers, real numbers, FAQPage schema.
  4. Then own EV charger queries. The dedicated EV page + Tesla / Qmerit pursuit is where the next 90 days of leverage lives — competitors haven't fully locked up the AI-search lane yet.
  5. Then layer in financing, schema, gallery, and named-expert content as ongoing improvements.

This is a build engagement, not a rescue. The credentials are already real; the surface area to display them is what comes next. The 90-day arc — site, schema, EV positioning, GBP rebuild, certifications — is what shifts both Google rankings and how often Stag surfaces when a Mesa homeowner asks ChatGPT or Perplexity, "who installs Tesla Wall Connectors near me?" or "panel upgrade Phoenix cost." Both answers are winnable.
